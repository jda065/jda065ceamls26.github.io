what_i_learned: |
 Today we began developing and testing machine learning models using our research datasets. For my asthma-focused dataset, we worked with the Extreme Learning Machine (ELM), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Random Forest models. I learned that each machine learning model processes data differently, which can lead to differences in prediction performance and accuracy. The purpose of applying these models is to analyze environmental and socioeconomic factors and determine how effectively they can be used to predict asthma-related outcomes. We began connecting the data preprocessing work we completed in previous sessions to the actual model-building process. This helped me better understand why cleaning, sorting, balancing, and preparing the data are important before training a machine learning model.
blockers: |
 One challenge I faced today was understanding the differences between the machine learning models and how each model makes its predictions. Since ELM, SVM, and Random Forest use different approaches, it was sometimes difficult to understand why one model may perform better than another. I also had to pay close attention to the code and model outputs to understand how our training data was being used to make predictions.
reflection: |
 Overall, today was a very interesting and productive day because we finally began applying machine learning directly to our research projects. After spending several days cleaning and preparing our data, it was exciting to see how that work connects to training predictive models. Working with ELM, SVM, and Random Forest gave me a better understanding of how machine learning can be used to analyze asthma-related data and identify patterns within large datasets. Although I still have more to learn about how each model works internally, I feel like I am beginning to understand the complete machine learning process. I am looking forward to comparing the performance of our models and seeing which approach provides the strongest predictions for asthma.
