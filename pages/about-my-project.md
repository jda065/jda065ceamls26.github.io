problem: |
          Respiratory illnesses such as asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and other breathing-related conditions affect           thousands of residents in Baltimore, particularly those living in areas with high levels of air pollution and environmental                 stressors. Exposure to poor air quality, traffic emissions, industrial pollutants, temperature extremes, and other urban                    environmental factors can increase the likelihood of respiratory complications and hospital visits. These health risks                      disproportionately affect vulnerable populations, including children, older adults, and individuals with preexisting medical                conditions. As cities continue to grow and environmental conditions change, identifying communities at greatest risk becomes                increasingly important for public health planning and prevention efforts.

          This project develops an optimization-driven machine learning framework that uses urban environmental data to predict respiratory           health risks across Baltimore. While many existing studies examine individual environmental factors, they often fail to integrate           multiple data sources or optimize predictive performance for local decision-making. The proposed framework addresses this gap by            combining environmental indicators with machine learning and optimization techniques to generate more accurate risk predictions.            By identifying patterns associated with elevated respiratory health risks, the project aims to support healthcare providers,                policymakers, and city planners in targeting interventions, allocating resources more effectively, and reducing preventable                 respiratory health outcomes.

approach: |
  

          Step 1 — Collect and prepare data
          Gather Baltimore urban environmental data such as air quality, temperature, humidity, traffic activity, population density, and             respiratory health records. Clean and organize the data using Python, Pandas, NumPy, and R.
          Step 2 — Build machine learning models
          Use models such as Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, Support Vector Machine, and Extreme Learning Machine to predict                   respiratory health risk levels based on the environmental factors.
          Step 3 — Optimize and evaluate the models
          Apply optimization and feature selection methods such as Forward Selection, Stepwise Selection, Genetic Algorithm, and Douglas              optimization to improve model performance. Test the models using metrics like RMSE, MAE, and NAE.
          Step 4 — Communicate the results
          Present the findings through charts, tables, and a clear explanation of which environmental factors were most connected to                  respiratory risk. Share the results in a final report, presentation slides, and visual summaries that can be understood by both             technical and non-technical audiences.
